[Shot Language Library]

Shot sizes:
- Extreme wide: grandeur, epic scale, tiny human presence
- Wide/full: establish environment and character relationships
- Medium: dialogue, interaction, daily actions
- Close: emotion and reaction
- Close detail: eyes, hands, key props
- Extreme close detail: pupil, lips, a single tear

Angles:
- Eye-level: daily, equal, natural
- Low angle: authority, grandeur (action/epic)
- High angle: smallness, global overview (large-scale scene)
- Over-shoulder: dialogue, confrontation
- Dutch angle: unease/tension (suspense)
- POV: immersion

Camera movement:
- Static: gaze, silence, calm dialogue
- Slow push/slow pull: emotional buildup, reveal, gentle transition
- Follow: character movement, walking
- Fast push/fast pull: shock, impact (tense scenes)
- Orbit/rise/dive: ritual, epic sensation
- Handheld shake: chaos, tension (action)

[Shot Style by scene_type]

daily (everyday/dialogue):
- Mainly medium and close, occasional close detail
- Mainly eye-level, alternating over-shoulder
- ? Prefer slow push/slow pull/light follow; avoid all-static coverage
- Movement wording: slowly pushes in, gently follows, slight sway, slow orbit
- Character behavior: even in dialogue scenes, add subtle actions (nod, turn head, gesture, step)

emotion (emotional/lyrical):
- Close and close detail to capture emotion
- Emotional peak may use extreme close detail
- ? Prefer slow push and orbit; avoid all-static coverage
- Movement wording: slowly pushes in, gently orbits, slight shake
- Character behavior: slight head raise, turn, lower head, wipe tears, walk toward window

action (combat/chase):
- Fast alternation of scales; close detail + wide intercut
- Low angle/high angle/Dutch angle for impact
- Fast push/pull, follow, handheld shake
- Movement wording: suddenly, rapidly, explosively

epic (grand scale):
- Must include extreme wide to establish scale
- High angle/rise/dive to show magnitude
- Place characters near frame edge to emphasize smallness
- Movement wording: slowly rises, rapidly dives, orbits

suspense (tense/mystery):
- POV and Dutch angle
- Slow forward pressure-building movement
- Sudden cuts to break rhythm

[Shot Continuity Rules]
- Shots must be continuous and coherent
- Within one sequence, progression should be gradual: wide->medium->close or close->medium->wide
- New scenes generally need an establishing wide shot
- Ensure diversity; avoid repetitive shot scales
- Keep visuals alive, not rigid

[video_prompt Writing Rules - Important]

Video models do not understand names. You must replace names with age group + gender:
- Format: age-gender + action + camera movement + environment
- Match movement intensity to scene type
- Do not add content absent from panel
- Motion scenes must be dynamic; static scenes still need expressive body language and facial cues
- If source indicates speaking, video_prompt must explicitly include "speaking"

[!] [Dynamic-first Principle - Core Rule]

Video must not be stiff. Every video_prompt must include movement:

1. Character action verbs (must use):
   - Head: turns head, nods, raises head, lowers head, tilts head, looks back
   - Hands: raises hand, waves, points, clenches fist, lowers hand, picks up, touches
   - Body: walks, turns, stands up, sits down, leans forward, steps back, moves closer
   - Expression: slight frown, slight smile, flickering gaze, softly smiling

2. Camera movement verbs (prefer these; avoid "static" by default):
   - Common: slowly pushes in, gently follows, slight sway, orbiting shot
   - Dynamic: handheld follow, subtle shake, slow orbit, rising high-angle
   - Intense: rapid push-in, fast follow, sudden pull-back, diving down

3. No purely static descriptions:
   ? Wrong: "Young woman sits on sofa, camera static"
   ? Correct: "Young woman sits on sofa and gently turns her head, camera slowly pushes toward her profile"

   ? Wrong: "Middle-aged man stands at door, serious expression"
   ? Correct: "Middle-aged man pushes door open and walks in, slight frown, handheld follow"

4. Even dialogue scenes must move:
   ? Wrong: "Young man speaks, static camera"
   ? Correct: "Young man speaks while gesturing and nodding slightly, camera slowly pushes in"

[!] Flashback/voiceover/inner-monologue rule:
- Do not output static pondering, idle staring, or empty inserts only
- video_prompt must show actual action and scene from the narrated content
- Visuals must be tightly bound to plot, not just "character standing and recalling"
- Example: narration says "their first encounter" -> depict concrete encounter actions

Age-group categories (use only these labels):
- teenage boy/teenage girl: about 10-16
- young man/young woman: about 17-30
- middle-aged man/middle-aged woman: about 31-50
- elderly man/elderly woman: above 50

[!] [Close-detail shots must use static camera]
- If shot type is close detail (hand detail, object detail, local close detail, etc.)
- video_prompt must explicitly say "static camera" or "camera remains fixed"
- Any camera movement is forbidden in close-detail shots
- Reason: close-detail frames show only partial area; movement may reveal unintended content

Examples (with dynamic elements):
- Daily dialogue: "A young woman lifts a coffee cup, blows lightly, and looks toward the window; sunlight falls on her profile; camera slowly pushes in"
- Action: "A teenage boy leaps and slashes in an arc, robe fluttering, handheld low-angle follow"
- Emotion: "A young woman slowly lowers her head, tears run down her cheeks, she lifts her hand to wipe her eyes, camera gently orbits"
- Dialogue: "A middle-aged man taps the table with his finger and speaks with a stern expression, camera has a subtle sway"
- Walking: "A young man strides down the street, wind lifting his coat hem, handheld follow"
- Close detail: "A hand slowly turns a page, fingertip gliding across text, static camera"
